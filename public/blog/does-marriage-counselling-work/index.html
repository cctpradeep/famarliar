Does marriage counselling work?

"It's for people with real problems." Or, "What is a stranger going to tell us that we don't already know?" Or just, "It doesn't work." One of you has said a sentence like that. The other is reading this late, wondering whether they are right.

We have heard every version of it, usually on the free call, usually from the partner who did not book it. Underneath it is a fair question. Counselling costs money and courage, and nobody wants to spend either on something that does not work.

So here is the honest answer, from two people who have sat on both sides of the room. We went for counselling ourselves before our wedding. A few years in, we hit seasons we could not have got through alone, and we were mentored through them. Thirteen years on we call our marriage joyful, and we now do this work for other couples. We are not neutral. We will try to be honest anyway.

Does marriage counselling work?

Yes, for most couples who do the work, and no counsellor can promise it for yours. The best long-term research finds that around seven in ten couples improve with couple therapy, and that many are still better years later. What it cannot do is work on one person's behalf, or repair a marriage one of you has already left.

We won't promise you that every marriage can be rebuilt, because that wouldn't be honest. What we can say is that couples who still care enough to be afraid usually have more to work with than they think.

The rest of this is what sits behind those two paragraphs: what "working" means, what the research actually found, what it looked like in our own marriage, and the situations where counselling is the wrong first step.

What does "working" actually mean?

Working means the argument stops running the house, the two of you learn how to disagree without one of you leaving the room, and you both know what you are doing next. It does not mean nobody ever fights again, and it does not mean staying together at any cost.

What working looks like What it does not mean
The same argument stops returning every few weeks Never disagreeing again
You can raise something hard without it becoming a war Your partner becoming a different person
You both know what you are working towards A counsellor deciding your marriage for you
Ordinary Tuesdays get easier Every day feeling like the first year
The two of you are on the same side of the problem Staying together whatever it costs you

That last line needs saying plainly. We believe far more can be repaired than people fear, and we have watched it happen. We will also never lean on anyone to stay where they are being harmed. Nobody in our room will tell you what to decide about your marriage. That decision is yours and it stays yours.

What does the research say, honestly?

It says counselling helps most couples and not all. Lebow and colleagues (2012) reviewed a decade of research for the Journal of Marital and Family Therapy. Their conclusion: couple therapy positively affects about seven couples in ten who receive it, with results comparable to individual therapy and far better than no treatment.

The longer view is more sobering and, read carefully, more hopeful. A study by Christensen and colleagues (2010), published by the Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology, followed 134 married couples described as chronically and seriously distressed for five years after about eight months of therapy. Five years on, roughly half were still clinically improved, and about a quarter had separated or divorced. These were couples in real trouble, and half of them were still doing better half a decade later without further help.

Two honest caveats. Both studies are Western, with couples in structured trials under conditions that do not look much like a practice in Byrathi. And we have no outcome figures of our own. We would rather tell you that than invent some. What we have is thirteen years of our own marriage and five years of sitting with couples in Bangalore, which is a different kind of evidence, and the only kind we will claim.

Did it work for us?

Yes, and not in one sitting. Before our wedding a counsellor showed us small things we were already doing to hurt each other without knowing it. A few years in, when the hard seasons came, we were mentored by people who knew what they were doing. We are not going to describe those seasons here. They are ours.

What we can describe is what we came out with, because it is the same four things we now work through with every couple.

Communication builds a marriage. Words. Tone. What the body is saying. And the gap between what one of us said and what the other heard.

Roles matter, and intention matters more. Who does what is less important than whether it was decided or merely assumed.

Cherish, don't just survive. A marriage that is only being managed is already losing something.

Know each other's love language. We credit Gary Chapman's The Five Love Languages for this, and it changed how we treat each other on ordinary days.

None of that arrived in a single session. It arrived across months, with a great deal of it happening at home in between, which is exactly how we run things for the couples we see now.

What makes counselling work?

Two people willing to say true things, in a room where it is safe to say them, with enough time between sessions to try what was said. In our experience that matters more than the method, the counsellor, or how bad things have become by the time you arrive.

Both of you in the room. One of you can start alone, and that is a real start. The work moves faster when both of you are there, because a marriage is worked on by the people in it.

Saying the true thing, in the language you argue in. We hold sessions in Tamil and Kannada as well as English. Couples say things in the language they grew up arguing in that they never quite manage in English, and the true version is the one we need.

The fortnight in between. Sessions sit ten to fifteen days apart on purpose. Something said in the room on a Saturday comes back on Wednesday evening without us, and that second conversation is where much of the change happens.

Coming before the pattern has years behind it. "We are always quarrelling, even though there is no specific reason." If that is your sentence, it is a good sign. It means the pattern has not yet been given a name, and naming it is where we begin.

Treating the household as part of the marriage. In many Bangalore homes the marriage shares a roof with parents, and the argument that keeps returning is often about them. We do not treat that as a side topic, because it is not one.

The assessment. Before the first session you each complete the Prepare/Enrich assessment separately, and it is paid once. The report shows where your answers agree and where they pull apart, so the first session starts somewhere useful rather than with you describing the marriage from scratch. There is more on how that works in what actually happens in premarital counselling, because engaged couples take the same one.

Here is a pattern we see often, described as a composite rather than any real couple. Married six years, living with his parents in Banaswadi, they arrive with "we are always quarrelling and there is no reason". The assessment shows they agree on almost everything except who decides what in the house, and neither has ever said the word "decide" out loud. By the third session the quarrel has a name. It does not vanish. It gets shorter, and it stops arriving at dinner in front of his mother. That is what working looks like at week six.

How long does it take to work?

Most couples notice the first change within the first block of five sessions, which run ten to fifteen days apart, so within about two months. That first change is in how you argue, not the end of every problem. After the block, couples carry on session by session for as long as it helps, and many need fewer than they feared.

A pattern with years behind it takes longer than one with months. It is still workable. What we ask is that you give it the block, because five sessions is the least it takes for something to be tried, fail, be tried differently and begin to hold.

When does marriage counselling not work?

When one of you has already decided to leave and wants a witness rather than a way back. When an affair is still going on in secret. When either of you comes to win. And in two situations where joint counselling is not the right first step at all: where there is violence, and where one of you is in active addiction.

Already decided. Counselling cannot rebuild a marriage on behalf of someone who has left it in their heart. If that is where one of you is, say so on the free call. It changes what we would suggest, and it is nothing to be ashamed of.

An affair still going on. The work stalls around anything one person is hiding. We do not force confessions. We do tell couples honestly when the room has stopped moving.

Coming to win. If the aim is to have two professionals confirm that your partner is the problem, the hour is wasted, and we will say so kindly.

Violence. If you are frightened of your partner, if there has been hitting, throwing, or threats, sitting in a room together is not the first step and can make things less safe. Call 181 or 112, or take our free call alone and we will point you to the right help. Nobody will ask you to sit across from someone you are afraid of.

Active addiction. Where drink or drugs are running one person's life, that person needs help of their own first. Couple work can follow, and often does. We can help you find the first kind.

Does marriage counselling work after an affair?

It can, and couples come to us for exactly this. It works when the affair is over and both people are willing to talk about what happened and about what was missing before it. It does not work while the affair continues, or while one person is expected simply to get over it. We will not tell you whether to stay. That is yours to decide.

Many people arrive suspecting rather than knowing. "I suspect there is something else going on" is a sentence we hear often, and it is a reason to talk, on your own if need be, rather than to wait for proof.

Does it work if only one of us wants to go?

It can start with one. You can take the free call alone, and either of us can meet you on your own. Counselling with one partner is not marriage counselling in the full sense, but it changes how that person shows up at home, and the other often comes in later as a sceptic and stays.

Scepticism is fine. We would rather have a sceptic in the room than an empty chair. Outright refusal is harder, and if that is what you are facing, the free call is the place to talk honestly about whether pushing is worth it.

Does online counselling work as well as in person?

In our experience, yes, provided both of you have a private hour. We work in person in Byrathi by preference, and online whenever a couple is in another city or country. What changes is the setting rather than the work: the door has to close, and a bedroom with parents in the next room is not always private enough for the true version of things.

Common questions

Will you tell us whether to stay together? No. Our work is to make sure you are deciding with the fullest possible picture and a way of talking to each other that holds. The decision stays yours.

Can counselling work if we have been like this for years? Yes. It takes longer than it would have, and it is still workable. Most couples who come to us have waited a long time, and many are surprised by how much remains once the pattern has a name.

How will we know it is working? By the third or fourth session the argument you keep having has a name, and you can talk about it without it starting. That is the first sign. The rest follows from it.

What if my partner thinks counselling means something is wrong with us? Something is wrong, or you would not be reading this, and that is no shame. Describe it as getting help with one argument rather than fixing the marriage. Most sceptics can agree to that, and the free call is a low way in.

Does it work in Tamil or Kannada? Yes. We counsel in both, as well as English, and for many couples the session in their own language is the one where the honest thing finally gets said.

Where to begin

Tonight, ask each other one question: what is the argument we keep having actually about? If neither of you can answer, that is not a bad sign. It is the question we would start with.

The fact that one of you is reading this is more than many marriages get. It is enough to begin with.
